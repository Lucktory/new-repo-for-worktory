06 — About

How I think about building software

I work across the whole stack on purpose. Most of the expensive problems in a product do not live in the frontend or the backend — they live in the seam between them, and in the gap between what the data actually looks like and what the interface pretends it looks like. Owning both ends is how those stop being someone else's problem.

I would rather ship something small that genuinely works than something large that almost does. A feature that is ninety percent finished is zero percent useful, so I cut scope early and visibly instead of quietly running out of time at the end.

Senior, to me, means owning the decision and not just the ticket — saying when a requested approach is the wrong one, and being specific about why.

Direct communication — no account manager relaying things in between
Written decisions, so the reasoning survives after the project does
Code handed over readable, so you are never locked to one developer
